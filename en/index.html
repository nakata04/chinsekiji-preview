About the Temple

Chinsekiji is a temple of Shinshū Ōtani-ha, a branch of Jōdo Shinshū (True Pure Land Buddhism), in Hitachiota, Ibaraki.

According to temple tradition, it was founded by Nyūsaibō Dōen (traditionally identified with Hino Saemon Yoriaki), a disciple of Shinran Shōnin. The temple is remembered as the fifteenth of the Twenty-four Disciples of Shinran.

The Legend of the Pillow Stone

According to temple tradition, on a snowy evening in 1212 (Kenryaku 2), Shinran Shōnin visited the village of Daimon and spent the night with a stone as his pillow.

Shinran Shōnin's Pillow Stone

According to temple tradition, this is the stone that gave the temple its name: Shinran Shōnin is said to have rested his head on it on a snowy night. The stone is traditionally described as bearing the characters 大 (Dai), 心 (Shin), and 海 (Kai).

Read more →

Principal Image: Standing Amida Buddha

According to temple tradition, this statue was donated in 1673 by Tokugawa Mitsukuni, the second lord of the Mito Domain. The statue itself is thought to date from the Kamakura period.

Read more →

Three Buddhist Sutras in Gold on Indigo Paper (Konshi Kindei Sanbu Myōten)

These Buddhist scriptures are written in gold ink on indigo-dyed paper. Of the original four volumes, three survive and are carefully preserved at the temple.
